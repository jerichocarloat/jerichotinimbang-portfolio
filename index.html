The quiet leak

Silent churn is the real revenue leak

Churn rarely announces itself. It shows up first as a broken repeat-purchase pattern: one skipped cycle, then two, until the number in your dashboard finally moves, and winning them back costs multiples of what retention would have.

01

They stop opening

Your emails still report as delivered. They simply stopped landing with anyone, and nothing in your reporting flags that as a problem.

02

They stop buying

One quiet month looks like noise. Three looks like a broken repeat-purchase pattern. By then, rebuilding the habit costs more than protecting it would have.

03

They never actually leave

They never cancel, so they never appear in a churn report. They sit in your database looking like customers while spending nothing.

Winning back a customer you already have costs a fraction of finding a new one. That gap is where I work.

Process

The loop I run, in the order I run it

Same six steps whether the goal is onboarding, retention or win-back. Below: the actual journey, the actual query, and the numbers they moved.

Jump to results
  1. 01

    Find the money that is already yours

    Not a blast to everyone. At Maya it was borrowers with a clean repayment record who had gone quiet for 90 days: proven buyers, zero acquisition cost, sitting idle.

  2. 02

    Make it survive a Finance review

    I model the upside, cap the incentive so it pays for itself on fees alone, and bring the numbers to Product and Finance. Campaigns that are not funded do not happen.

  3. 03

    Design for the inbox, not the dashboard

    Four messages, not fourteen. In-app, push, SMS then email, with buyers exiting the moment they convert and a hold-out group deliberately left untouched.

  4. 04

    Write the query myself

    Repayment history, recency, consent, all filtered in SQL against real behavior. No ticket, no queue, and no segment I cannot defend in a meeting.

  5. 05

    Ship it properly

    Built in CleverTap with the conversion goal wired up. Frequency caps and quiet hours are set, and two versions of the offer run against each other from day one.

  6. 06

    Prove the lift was real

    Hold-out versus treated, so the number survives scrutiny. Then I change one thing, timing, channel or offer, and run it again.

The cohort, straight from Databricks

-- Quiet but valuable Maya Advance borrowers
WITH last_loan AS (
  SELECT customer_id,
         MAX(loan_date)       AS last_loan_date,
         COUNT(*)              AS lifetime_loans,
         SUM(principal_amount) AS lifetime_gtv,
         AVG(days_past_due)    AS avg_dpd
  FROM  fact_maya_advance_loans
  WHERE status = 'PAID'
  GROUP BY customer_id
)
SELECT c.customer_id, c.msisdn, c.email, l.lifetime_gtv,
       DATEDIFF(CURRENT_DATE, l.last_loan_date) AS days_quiet
FROM  last_loan l
JOIN  dim_customer c USING (customer_id)
WHERE DATEDIFF(CURRENT_DATE, l.last_loan_date) BETWEEN 90 AND 365
  AND l.avg_dpd <= 7
  AND l.lifetime_loans >= 2
  AND c.marketing_consent = TRUE
ORDER BY l.lifetime_gtv DESC;

FAQ

The objections, answered before you have to ask

You have two years of experience. Why should I take the numbers seriously?

Because they are dated, converted, and measured against hold-out groups, and because you can check the parts that are checkable. Two years is two years: I am not going to claim I have run a department. What I have done is own a lifecycle loop end to end at a fintech with millions of users, and the method transfers even where the scale does not. Treat the numbers as evidence of process, not of seniority.

What kind of role are you looking for?

CRM, lifecycle or retention marketing at a SaaS or product company. The work I am proudest of sits exactly there: finding a segment in the data, building the journey, and proving the revenue. Titles vary, the job is the same.

Can you really write your own SQL, or do you need a data team?

I write it myself. The cohort query in the process section above is mine, pulled from Databricks. It means I can test an idea the same afternoon I have it rather than joining a queue, and I never hand over a segment I cannot explain.

Do you only do CRM, or the wider funnel too?

CRM and lifecycle is where I am strongest, but I have run paid media, rebuilt landing pages and designed the creative. That usually helps: I can fix the ad, the page and the follow up instead of filing a ticket for each.

How do you know a campaign actually worked?

A hold-out group. A random slice of the audience is deliberately left alone so the comparison is real, not just a before and after screenshot. If the lift is not there, I would rather find out and change the offer.

Your experience is fintech and agency. We are B2B SaaS. Does that transfer?

The mechanics do, and they are the hard part. A dormant borrower and a trial user who stopped logging in are the same problem wearing different clothes: find the segment behaviorally, work out what reactivation is worth, build a journey that respects the inbox, prove the lift against a hold-out. What changes in SaaS is the vocabulary and the event model, activation and seats and expansion instead of transfers and GTV, plus a longer feedback loop because contracts renew annually rather than transacting weekly. That is a few weeks of learning your data model, not a career change. The two case studies on this site are me doing exactly that translation unprompted.

Have you owned a full CRM stack, or only executed inside one?

Owned the loop, not the procurement. At Maya I built segments in Databricks, orchestrated journeys in CleverTap, wired conversion goals and guardrails, and reported incrementality to Product and Finance. What I have not done is select and implement a CRM platform from scratch, or manage a CRM team. If the role needs either on day one, I would rather you know that now.

How do you work with engineering and data teams?

By arriving with the query already written. Most friction between CRM and engineering comes from vague asks, so I bring the segment definition, the events I need fired, and why. At Maya that meant working through Kyuubi and JIRA with Product and Engineering on the marketplace migration, which is also how I learned to tell the difference between a marketing problem and a plumbing problem.

What would you want in the first week?

Read access to the warehouse, the event taxonomy, the current journey inventory with message volume per user, and the retention curve by cohort. From those four things I can usually tell you where the money is leaking before the end of the week, which is what the 30-day plan above is built on.

Where are you based, and can you work with our timezone?

Budapest, Hungary, so Central European Time. I have worked to Australian hours at 33 Bondi and Philippine hours at Maya, so overlap with the Americas or APAC is familiar rather than theoretical. Open to remote or hybrid, available now, and I usually reply the same day.
